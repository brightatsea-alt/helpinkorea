Do you need a Korea local agent under PIPA? A 5-minute check

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read · Written by a Korea-based compliance professional

Short answer: if your company has no office or address in Korea but collects personal data from people in Korea, and you are above certain size thresholds, Korean law requires you to appoint a domestic representative (국내대리인, often called a “local agent”). Below is who it applies to, what the agent actually does, and what happens if you ignore it.

What is a “domestic representative” in Korea?

A domestic representative is a person or company located in Korea, appointed in writing by an overseas data controller, to act on its behalf toward Korean data subjects and the regulator (the Personal Information Protection Commission, PIPC). The concept is close to the EU GDPR Article 27 representative. The appointment must be disclosed in your Korean-facing privacy policy with the representative's name, address, phone number and email.

Who has to appoint one?

The duty sits in Article 31-2 of the Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) and its Enforcement Decree. As currently written, it applies to a personal-information controller without an address or place of business in Korea that meets any of the following:

TriggerThreshold (check current decree)
Total revenuePrior-year worldwide revenue of KRW 1 trillion or more
Information & communication service revenuePrior-year revenue from such services of KRW 10 billion or more
Korean usersDaily average of 1,000,000+ Korean users over the last 3 months of the prior year
Regulator orderOrdered by the PIPC after an investigation
Not above the thresholds? Then the appointment is not mandatory today — but many mid-size SaaS, gaming and e-commerce companies appoint one voluntarily because Korean enterprise customers and app-store reviewers increasingly ask for a Korean contact in the privacy policy, and because the thresholds are periodically lowered. It is cheap insurance.

What does the local agent actually do?

  • Receives and forwards complaints, access and deletion requests from Korean users.
  • Receives official correspondence from the PIPC (and KISA), and coordinates your response within statutory deadlines.
  • Handles breach-notification support and, where required, files reports in Korean on your behalf.
  • Is named in your privacy policy as your Korean point of contact.

The agent is your representative — liability for compliance stays with your company. A good agent therefore insists on a service agreement that defines what it will and will not sign, and carries professional liability insurance.

What are the penalties?

Failing to appoint a domestic representative when required is an administrative fine (과태료) of up to KRW 20 million per violation. The bigger risk is practical: without a Korean contact, PIPC letters go unanswered, deadlines are missed, and a routine inquiry becomes a formal investigation with penalty surcharges that scale with revenue.

Frequently asked

Can our Korean law firm be the agent? Yes, and for large companies that is common. For most SMEs a dedicated agent service is 5–10× cheaper for what is mostly a mailbox-and-escalation role, with the law firm brought in only when something actually happens.

Does the agent need to be a Korean company? The agent must have an address in Korea. It can be an individual or a company.

We already have a GDPR representative in the EU. Does that count? No. The Korean appointment is separate and must be a Korean address.

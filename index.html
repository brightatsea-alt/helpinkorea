FAQ

Questions we get every week

What is a Korea “local agent” or “domestic representative”, and do I need one?
It is a person or company in Korea legally appointed to act for a foreign business. Korea's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA) and Network Act require many overseas online services without a Korean office to appoint one; cosmetics, medical devices, food and trademarks each need a Korea-based responsible party or agent as well. If you sell to Korean users or import regulated goods, the answer is usually yes — send us your situation and we'll confirm within a day.
Can you actually sign as our representative, or do you only advise?
We act. For PIPA/Network Act we are named as your domestic representative. For MFDS, customs and trademarks we coordinate licensed partners (responsible-party holders, customs brokers, patent attorneys) so that each filing is made by the party the law requires, under one point of contact — us.
How fast do you reply, and in which time zone?
Within one business day, Korea time (UTC+9). Urgent matters — a regulator letter, a shipment held at customs — get a same-day WhatsApp response.
How do we pay from abroad?
USD invoices payable by PayPal, Wise or international wire. Retainers and annual agent fees are invoiced in advance; concierge tasks are paid per task via PayPal. Korean clients can pay in KRW by bank transfer.
Do you sign an NDA and a proper contract?
Always. An English-language service agreement and NDA define scope, fees, liability limits and termination before any work starts. We are happy to work on your paper if you prefer.
Which industries do you know best?
Shipping, ship management, marine equipment, energy and industrial suppliers — that is where our own career is. We also handle software/SaaS local-agent appointments and consumer brands (cosmetics, food) through partners.
What happens when we open our own Korean office later?
Good news — that is the goal. We hand over contacts, files and relationships in a structured transition, and can help recruit or act as interim country manager. The transition terms are written into the agreement from day one, so there are no surprises.
